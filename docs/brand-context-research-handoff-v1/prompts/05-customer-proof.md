# Customer evidence and review task

Research named customer stories, attributable testimonials and relevant reviews for the exact company/products. The host may request the existing reviews document or an expanded usable-proof document; do not conflate the two. A logo wall is a lead to investigate, not proof of product usage or outcomes.

For each important case capture the actual customer entity, operator/business unit, source date, operating situation, problem/workaround, named product or supported capability, reported result, baseline, denominator, measurement period and limitations. If title/logo and narrative name different entities, resolve or preserve the ambiguity. Record whether a result is measured, company-reported, customer-reported, expected, modeled or aspirational. Do not silently change “could save” into “saved.”

Preserve cost scope: software-only, managed service, staffed operation and combined program results are different. A quoted percentage must retain its definition and period; a future target must not become proof. Identify contradictory hero/body statistics. Reviews establish the reviewer's experience, not universal performance; retain edition, date and role when known. Avoid treating syndicated copies as independent corroboration.

Write: Proof by customer and buying situation; What the results do and do not establish; Matching proof to a campaign; Evidence gaps. Use a customer/situation → supported claim → scope and caveat → source table, with narrative for consequential conflicts. Link evidence to the corresponding product and proposed segment only when the source supports the relation.

For campaign use, give safe attributed claims and the missing permission or substantiation questions, without approving publication. Report evidence thinness rather than manufacturing hotel, enterprise or vertical coverage to balance a table. This research supplies proof to other tasks; it does not set the company's ICP strategy by customer-count popularity alone.
