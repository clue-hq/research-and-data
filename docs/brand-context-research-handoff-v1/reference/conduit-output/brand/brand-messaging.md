## Core positioning

**Observed company language.** The shortest public line is “AI agents for hospitality.” The homepage expands it: answer guest messages and calls, handle requests end to end, and coordinate work across teams. The company page calls Conduit “the AI agent platform for hospitality”: a fleet of on-brand agents across every channel that convert inquiries into bookings, drive upsell revenue, run internal operations, and surface insights, connected to systems operators already use. The manifesto uses a sharper frame—“the coordination layer for hospitality”—an agent that works the same channels an operator does and acts on the business’s behalf instead of only shuttling messages. Industry nav is short-term rentals, independent hotels, and hotel groups. The product system behind that claim is [AI Agents](#knowledge/product-agents), [Inbox](#knowledge/product-inbox), [Guest Portals](#knowledge/product-portals), and [Workflows](#knowledge/product-workflows), with Operations labeled coming soon. Identity, founding-year, and brand-count conflicts belong in [Company overview](#knowledge/company-overview); capability packaging belongs in [Product catalog & capability map](#knowledge/products-capabilities). [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/) [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/company) [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/manifesto)

**Recommended articulation (draft, not a published slogan).** For a guest-operations buyer: Conduit is the hospitality-specific agent layer that answers guests on the channels they already use, takes the next step in connected tools, and hands exceptions to your team with the thread intact. Keep manifesto “coordination layer” language for thought leadership. Keep “AI agents for hospitality” for awareness. Do not invent a new category name.

## Promise and supporting pillars

**Observed promise.** Agents answer with property information and reservation data, act through connected tools (for example updating a booking, collecting payment, or creating a task), and escalate when a person is needed. Inbox brings messages and calls into one workspace with history. Portals give guests one branded stay link for arrival details, extras, payment, and an AI concierge. Workflows schedule confirmations, check-in, follow-ups, and rule-based upgrades. Homepage supporting claims include multilingual replies, inspectable agent activity, scheduled workflows, and reporting on automation rate, response time, resolution, and guest satisfaction. ## Why this work matters

The manifesto’s commercial argument is that hospitality already solved message speed; the bottleneck is how fast a person can absorb volume and act, especially after hours and across inboxes that do not talk to each other. It illustrates with attributed customer data: median human response on one team ranged from 26 minutes to 46 hours, versus an agent answer in 93 seconds. Hiring VAs is framed as adding people to orchestration, not removing it. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/manifesto)

Named stories make that concrete without treating them as typical. Haven reports moving from about 40% to 90% automation across roughly 270 properties, with support need falling from about 25 people to two per shift after teaching workflows. HomeHop describes portfolio growth from 40 to 108 listings without extra guest-support hires, but the customer-since header and two-year narrative disagree; do not present that growth as a verified Conduit-period result. During its first property-management acquisition, Cascadia’s leadership expected to absorb the additional work without more guest-service hires; the story does not establish a completed staffing outcome. Lauderdale’s story describes support spend beginning at $4,000, later reaching $1,200 on prior services, then $600/month with Conduit; the $4,000-to-$600 headline is not a clean immediate before/after. Its reported response time fell from 57 to 2 minutes with an AI platform and 24/7 staffed support team; the software-only contribution is not isolated. These stories mix attributed results and expectations, not a rate card or guaranteed outcomes. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/customers/haven-2026) [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/customers/homehop) [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/customers/cascadia-getaways) [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/customers/lauderdale-hotel)

Recommended emphasis in commercial copy: growth without linear headcount, after-hours coverage, and one thread for guest plus booking—not “AI changes everything.”

## Positioning boundaries

Stay inside the supported hospitality guest-operations use case: the site’s main industry groups plus serviced apartments, aparthotels, resorts and villas where their operating model fits. Do not pitch Conduit as a PMS, channel manager, generic helpdesk, or a substitute for people on the property. Operations is “coming soon” in the product nav even where stories mention maintenance or cleaning workflows—do not sell the coming-soon SKU as generally available. Do not promise 90% automation, $500k asset value, or 87% resolution as expected results. Do not use conflicting brand counts in one asset. Bocobay’s Intercom move and Flex’s stated choice not to rebuild conversational AI are different evidence types: Bocobay is a named replacement, while Flex is an operator’s build-versus-buy account. Neither establishes that Conduit beats every competitor. [Flex story](https://www.conduit.ai/customers/the-flex) For wording rules see [Brand voice & language](#knowledge/brand-voice) and [Audience-to-message map](#knowledge/message-map).

**Internal Agents, connector actions and Operations are separate.** Internal Agents are marketed for internal tasks; an action such as creating a housekeeping task depends on the connected system and permissions. Operations is a separately named product marked coming soon in navigation, even though Full Suite lists operational capabilities.
