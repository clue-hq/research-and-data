# Zafran research quality: Grok 4.6 medium, Muse Spark 1.3 max, DeepSeek v4 Pro high

This compares the actual saved account, Nick Fisher and Vari Kaur claim dossiers, acquired provider passages and initial narrative exports from the isolated 28 September runs. **Grok 4.6 is the strongest current baseline for the complete research-to-page task; Muse max is a serious research alternative with some uniquely rich person findings; DeepSeek high produced useful evidence but did not demonstrate equivalent fidelity or end-to-end completion.** This is one account and two fixed people, not a general model ranking.

The basis is 20 meaningful evidence probes below, not finding counts. A source containing a statement supports preservation/attribution review; it does not independently prove the source statement true. This is a targeted audit, not an exhaustive omission percentage. Detailed excerpts, exact finding objects, source references and absolute local paths are in [quality-evidence.json](quality-evidence.json).

## What the evidence supports

**Grok 4.6 medium best preserves immediately useful sales-research detail across the completed artifact set.** Its account dossier retains the complete BDR automation chain, ABM operating requirements, and named product mechanisms. Nick's dossier adds the concrete AI-risk warning, two distinctive Black Hat campaigns, book/hobby/audience evidence, and an actual transcribed interview. All three initial narratives were saved. Weaknesses remain: its account author omitted several valuable acquired statements that Nick later recovered, its narratives are lengthy, and several bookkeeping/bundling/entity-scope defects persist.

**Muse max cannot reasonably be dismissed as weak person research.** Its Nick investigation independently expands an old product-marketing article and Slideshare deck into meaningful first-person principles about empathy, credibility and storytelling. It follows CocktailGPT and verifies Vari's credential pages. Those are valuable discoveries that the other two completed dossiers did not supply. However, its account investigation misses later adverse reporting, preserves a stale CPO title and turns hiring into an unsupported “inconsistent with retrenchment” conclusion. The initial account page is shorter but less faithful to exact quote presentation, and neither contact narrative was published.

**DeepSeek high retains important workflow and personal evidence at lower recorded dollar cost, but this run is not a successful cheaper substitute.** It compresses away the same concrete AI-risk language even though it was returned, constructs an unsupported reporting hierarchy, and produces only the account and Nick dossiers. Vari's early stop and all missing narratives prevent a complete quality comparison. Its apparent lower total cost also reflects incomplete work. Runtime failures are not evidence that every DeepSeek research statement is poor.

## Particularly revealing comparisons

**A useful quote is not interchangeable with its topic.** All three retrieved Nick's AI adoption material. DeepSeek preserved “guardrails” and the playbook, but omitted the exact adverse actions. Grok's Nick F025 preserves “overwrite your pipeline data or blast prospects with something no human would ever send.” Muse's Nick F035 preserves overwrite risk and company-context advice, but not the whole prospect-blast example. This is a real selection/fidelity difference independent of search luck.

**The account/person handoff can recover account omissions without making the initial account complete.** Grok's account lacked the guardrail and creative-event statements even though sources were available; Nick later preserved them. Muse's Nick later recovered the complete BDR automation flow absent from the account. Those are benefits of separate person research, but an initial account page can still be misleading until reconciliation incorporates them. Muse's initial page calls BDR account research “manual” while the later person dossier shows a working automated intent-routing flow. A job description naming research does not establish that it is entirely manual.

**Discovery and interpretation failures must be separated.** Muse's saved acquisition scan did not find the CPO-departure reporting present in Grok and DeepSeek. That is a discovery/freshness gap. Calling hiring “inconsistent with retrenchment” is a separate reasoning error: even excellent hiring coverage cannot establish absence of distress. A qualifier about limited search does not repair that inference in factual text.

**More findings are not automatically more useful.** Muse's larger Nick dossier contains uniquely strong authored philosophy, but also non-authorship/non-appearance notes and colleague tools. DeepSeek's smaller Nick dossier still preserves meaningful book/hobby and GTM quotes. We should reward useful distinct evidence, correct ownership and source fidelity, not token volume or claim count.

**Media depth is a real differentiator, with a confound.** Grok reached actual ISMG speech and stored meaningful interview answers with host attribution and ASR qualifications. Muse attempted resolution and was refused; DeepSeek did not perform transcription. This supports “Grok delivered deeper media evidence in this run,” not “Muse cannot understand transcripts.”

**No run solved source-to-dossier coverage.** All three returned the Sanaz “5X” source wording and omitted it. It is an ambiguous reported metric, so it should not be fabricated into ARR; its ambiguity is not a reason to silently lose the public statement. All three also retain some bundling or entity-scope weaknesses.

## Narrative review boundary

The comparison uses each researcher's **initial account page**, preserved before the shared Muse reconciler, so Grok is not credited for a different model's later edits. Grok's contact pages exist; Muse's contact draft or failed write is not equivalent to a published page; DeepSeek has none. Missing pages are completion outcomes, not evidence of poor prose.

Grok's initial account page has stronger direct quotations and explicit “listing does not prove vacancy/procurement” caveats, but its long ICP-gate discussion, repeated sources and Tier-1 Q1 terminology remain unsuitable for the concise user experience requested. Muse's shorter page improves scanability but weakens quote fidelity and makes some stronger conclusions. Neither represents a finished editorial standard solely because the schema imported.

## Comparability and selection decision

- Same seller/ICP/persona context, fixed target people and revision-24 research resources; fresh isolated target state for each run.
- Different requested efforts: Grok **medium**, Muse **max**, DeepSeek **high**. These labels are provider-specific, not equivalent reasoning budgets.
- Search/deep results differ naturally. We label returned-but-omitted evidence separately from evidence not acquired.
- Later launches contain captured working-tree differences. This is not a perfectly bit-identical model-only experiment.
- The confirmed `lookup_person` parent-reference overwrite affects Nick in DeepSeek and both people in Muse. It erases delegation metadata and breaks parent-dossier/finalization checks; that cannot be scored solely as model incompetence.
- Grok's person finalization failures instead included account-owned committee references. Their dossiers and narratives survived; job failure does not erase delivered quality.
- All use the common Muse reconciler. Its successful Grok update and failed Muse/DeepSeek schema outputs are separate pipeline outcomes.

**Practical decision:** keep Grok 4.6 medium as the quality baseline now. Preserve Muse max as a candidate to retest after the shared reference and narrative contract failures are fixed; its unique person findings warrant that test. Do not select DeepSeek solely from the cheaper incomplete total. First compare fixed acquired-source replays for fidelity, then repeated fresh research runs for discovery. The current shared skills need stronger, general review behavior around source-specific public wording, uncertainty and entity scope regardless of model; the probes above are evaluation cases, not names or facts to hardcode into prompts.
