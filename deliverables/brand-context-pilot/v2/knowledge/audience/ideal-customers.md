Conduit’s public ICP is a hospitality **account**, not a jobs list. The pricing FAQ says Conduit is for hotel teams, serviced-apartment operators, and short-term rental managers, with AI agents that answer guest messages and calls, handle routine requests, and coordinate work with the team. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/pricing) Global navigation names three industries in this order: short-term rentals, independent hotels, hotel groups. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/product/agents)

**Fit** means the company operates guest stays, can supply property-specific policies and reservation context, and runs conversations on channels Conduit unifies, with humans for handoffs. A motivated buyer outside that operating model does not create fit. Investigation priority follows evidence strength in [Customer evidence & usable proof](#knowledge/customer-evidence), not invented market size: named stories cluster in vacation-rental / property-management companies; independent hotels have one boutique P&L write-up plus an industry page; hotel groups have product copy without a named group case body in this pack; serviced apartments are named in the FAQ only.

## Who is a good-fit account

A good-fit account is an operator of stays whose guest work is communication-heavy and **property-specific**. Agents use the operator’s property information and policies, act through supported connected tools, and follow rules for what the team should handle. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/product/agents) Inbox assumes conversations can be organized by property or team, with stay and booking context from connected PMS and OTA accounts beside the thread. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/product/inbox)

Hotels and vacation-rental operators are both in-category and **not the same test**. Independent-hotel pages stress after-hours calls, front-desk load, Opera / Mews / Cloudbeds, and extras such as room upgrades, spa, and dining. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/industry/independent-hotels) Short-term rental pages stress per-listing knowledge, Airbnb / Vrbo / Booking.com / Expedia threads, check-in instructions, and booking-change requests. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/industry/short-term-rentals) Hotel-group pages add shared brand standards with per-property policies and reservation details. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/industry/hotel-groups) Run [ICP · hotel operators](#knowledge/icp-hotels) and [ICP · vacation-rental operators](#knowledge/icp-rentals) as separate account tests. Serviced-apartment operators belong in the same FAQ sentence; investigate them as in-category until disproven—do not reject for a missing named story.

Company reach copy (“300+ hospitality brands in 120+ countries”) is a Conduit claim, not named-account proof. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/industry/independent-hotels)

## Buyer roles versus company fit

Company fit is the lodging model and guest-operations stack. Buyer roles are how that account buys and runs the product. Founders, CEOs, guest-services leads, and CX heads appear in stories; those titles are not the ICP. Map influence in [Buying committee & professional personas](#knowledge/buyer-roles). If the company does not operate hotel, serviced-apartment, or short-term rental stays, a strong champion does not create fit. If the company is in-category, a missing named persona is a research gap, not a reject.

## Readiness and urgency

Readiness the sources actually describe: connected channels and PMS/OTA context; a knowledge base of property policies; escalation rules; people who review handoffs. Haven’s story says Conduit was already installed at about 40% automation with listings uploaded, no workflows, and minimal knowledge-base work until the team standardized answers. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/customers/haven-2026) Docs-style product FAQ copy says how much agents resolve depends on guest requests, knowledge base, integrations, and escalation rules. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/product/agents.md)

Urgency is not a published score. Named stories involve growth, staffing, acquisitions, overnight coverage, and owner valuation. Test whether those situations exist with [Qualification & account research playbook](#knowledge/buying-triggers). Do not invent a buying attitude or timeline. Package choice (Messaging vs Guest vs devices vs Full Suite) is commercial mapping in [Pricing & packaging](#knowledge/pricing) after the account is in-category. Capabilities live in [Product catalog & capability map](#knowledge/products-capabilities).

## Exclusions and unknowns

Sources do not publish a negative ICP. Do not reject for unknown unit count, missing hotel-group proof, or an integration that is only a logo—investigate.

The practical out-of-category signal is an account that does not operate hotel, serviced-apartment, or short-term rental guest stays. That follows the FAQ; it is not a legal exclusion list. Hybrid real-estate-plus-hospitality operators that still run guest stays (Wynwood) remain in-category.

Material gaps: named proof for hotel groups and serviced apartments; whether “unit” equals listing, room, or key; franchise buying units; language product limits; whether Operations (nav: coming soon) is required for group ops use cases. Preserve the contradiction that Full Suite already lists workforce/operations lines while the product nav still says Operations is coming soon. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/pricing)

## What the company must confirm

Before treating segments as equally proven, confirm: hotel-group and serviced-apartment referenceability; billable unit vs listing vs room; which PMS/OTA paths actually write bookings, tasks, or payments for this stack; which required guest languages and channels are supported at the needed quality; and whether the contracting party is owner, management company, or brand. Until then, prioritize STR/PM investigation, keep independent hotels in-category with thinner proof, and treat hotel groups as marketed but under-evidenced.
