## Account definition

**Independent hotels** are marketed as property-level guest operations: agents answer calls and messages while the team looks after people at the desk, using that hotel’s policies and booking details, including after hours. The buying locus in the copy is the desk—front office, reservations, overnight cover—not a corporate shared-services org. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/industry/independent-hotels)

**Hotel groups** are marketed as a portfolio workspace: handle messages and calls across the group, share brand standards, and still give agents each property’s policies and reservation details (parent + child listings, shared knowledge base, org structure). Calls are described as landing in a shared inbox. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/industry/hotel-groups)

A site claim of being trusted by 300+ hospitality brands in 120+ countries is company reach language, not named hotel-group adoption. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/industry/hotel-groups)

The only named **hotel** customer body in this pack is The Lauderdale Boutique Hotel (using Conduit since December 2024; founder Noel Poler). [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/customers/lauderdale-hotel)

That is one boutique, hands-off-investment story—not a typical hotel outcome. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/customers/lauderdale-hotel)

**Scenario A — good-fit (hypothetical, unlabeled property):** A single independent hotel whose night team still takes late-arrival calls and WhatsApp, and a GM willing to maintain property policies. Opera or Mews would be readiness examples to investigate, not mandatory fit criteria. The job is coverage plus reservation context, not removing the desk.

**Scenario B — uncertain (hypothetical):** A group whose RFP requires labor/payroll or portfolio housekeeping as the must-have needs feature-level availability confirmation. **Scenario C — poor-fit (hypothetical):** A buyer who needs a physical self-check-in kiosk. Group copy: Conduit supports guest communication; it is not a physical check-in kiosk. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/industry/hotel-groups)

## Qualification decisions

Qualify **subsegment → communication job → stack → package → proof**, in that order.

1. Independent hotel vs hotel group vs mis-tagged STR or serviced apartment.
2. Buying locus: one desk and one policy set vs shared standards plus per-property exceptions.
3. Channels: voice and messaging in one workspace; confirm each OTA’s actual messaging versus reservation-context path. The integration directory has coming-soon labels, while other public pages describe OTA messaging; resolve that conflict for this account. [Integration directory](https://www.conduit.ai/integrations). [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/product/inbox)
4. Package vs job: Messaging vs Guest (portals, waivers, upsells) vs devices vs Full Suite—see [Pricing & packaging](#knowledge/pricing) and [Product catalog & capability map](#knowledge/products-capabilities).
5. Proof match: cite Lauderdale only for boutique owner-operator support cost, response time, and modeled asset value, with Conduit attribution and cap-rate caveats. No hotel-group case body is in this pack. Missing group proof means investigate, not reject. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/customers/lauderdale-hotel)

## Signals and next questions

Useful public signals: hotel (not listing) inventory; night phone plus OTA/WhatsApp; PMS names that appear on hotel pages; one GM vs brand standards team. None of these are numeric thresholds.

Ask: Which system is source of truth for reservations, and should guest messaging stay on the PMS? Who owns brand voice versus property exceptions? Which requests must still hit a person at the desk? Is the purchase guest communication, or housekeeping/labor still marked coming soon?

## Disqualifiers and unresolved assumptions

A physical check-in kiosk as the required deliverable is a product mismatch. Operational requirements such as housekeeping or workforce management require feature-level confirmation: navigation says Operations is coming soon while Full Suite lists operational capabilities. That conflict is a readiness question, not an automatic rejection of the hotel account. Separately, transferring STR percentages to hotels or assuming all PMS connectors support the same writes are sales errors, not negative ICP criteria. [Pricing](https://www.conduit.ai/pricing)

Unresolved in sources: no named hotel-group outcomes; Lauderdale’s $600/month and 2-minute average mix AI with a staffed support team, so the human share is not isolated; Guest includes portals at a published starting package rate, while the Portals FAQ also describes a listing-based add-on; confirm the applicable commercial path. No Portals outcome is isolated in the hotel story; implementation effort for hotels is not published here. [Source](https://www.conduit.ai/customers/lauderdale-hotel)
