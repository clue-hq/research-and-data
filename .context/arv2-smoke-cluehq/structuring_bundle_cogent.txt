1. You may ONLY use a field_key from the catalog given to you. If a fact does not fit any catalog

2. value_json MUST validate against that field's value_schema exactly (right JSON type, required

3. evidence_span MUST be a VERBATIM substring of the material given to you — copy the quoted text
   exactly as it appears. Never paraphrase into evidence_span. If there is no quote for a fact,
   omit the fact entirely.
4. source_url MUST be the URL the material attributes that fact to. Every row needs one. Never
   invent a URL and never reuse an unrelated one.
5. NEVER emit a row for anything marked "NOT FOUND". Never emit an automated/LinkedIn REVENUE
   ESTIMATE as core.annual_revenue — an estimate is not a reported number; skip it or use
   core.revenue_band only if the material states a band.
6. NEVER emit special-category personal data (health, religion, politics, sexual orientation,
   ethnicity, union membership, criminal matters, family/bereavement, home address, personal
   phone/email).
7. Do NOT emit provenance fields (source_key, tier, extraction_method, verification_status): the
   caller stamps those. Emit only field_key, value_json, evidence_span, source_url, confidence,
   priority, and for contact.facet.* also use_class.
8. Prefer fewer, well-evidenced rows over many thin ones. One row per field per distinct fact.

# Cogent Security Raises $42M Series A

Six months after launch, Cogent raises $42M led by Bain Capital Ventures to scale the first AI taskforce for vulnerability management.

Feb 18, 2026, 2 min read, Vineet Edupuganti, Co-Founder and CEO

Six months ago, we launched Cogent with a simple thesis: the hardest part of vulnerability management isn't finding the problems. It's fixing them.

Today, I'm excited to share that we've raised $42 million in Series A funding led by Bain Capital Ventures, with continued support from Greylock Partners and Definition. Founders and executives from OpenAI, Abnormal Security, and Datadog also invested personally. This brings our total funding to $53 million.

I'm especially proud to welcome [Enrique Salem](https://www.linkedin.com/in/enriquesalem/) to our Board of Directors. Enrique helped build and scale some of the defining security companies of the last two decades, including Symantec, Rubrik, and Netskope. He knows what it takes to turn a fast-growing security company into a lasting one, and we're lucky to have him.

## Why We're Here

The vulnerability management crisis has gotten worse since we launched. Over 48,000 new CVEs were published in the past year alone, 162 percent more than five years ago. And the window between a vulnerability being disclosed and an attacker exploiting it has collapsed, in part because attackers now use AI to move faster than ever.

Most security teams already have tools that find these issues. That's not the bottleneck. The bottleneck is everything after discovery: figuring out who owns the affected system, understanding why this specific vulnerability matters in this specific environment, getting the right fix in front of the right engineer, and confirming it actually got done. That work is still manual. It still involves chasing people through Slack threads and outdated spreadsheets. And it still takes days or weeks while attackers move in minutes.

Security teams don't have a visibility problem. They have an execution problem. That's the gap we built Cogent to close.

## What Cogent Does

Our AI agents handle the full lifecycle of vulnerability remediation. They investigate findings in the context of your actual environment, determine who owns what, assess real-world risk, generate remediation steps, and track fixes through to verified completion. Every action is traceable and auditable. Everything runs through configurable approval gates. Organizations deploy autonomous agents without giving up control.

Customers using Cogent have reduced their exposure windows for critical risks by an average of 97 percent. We're already working with dozens of Fortune 1000 companies and marquee brands across financial services, higher education, retail, and more.

## The Team

What I'm most proud of is the team we've assembled.

Our co-founders lived this problem firsthand. Geng and I came from Abnormal Security, where we helped scale the company from a small startup to a multibillion-dollar business. Thanos led infrastructure at Coinbase, where he ran large-scale vulnerability remediation programs. We've fused security product leaders from Abnormal, Zscaler, and Wiz with world-class AI and software talent from DeepMind, Coinbase, Databricks, Tesla, and Stripe. Our team includes AI researchers who helped develop the transformer architectures behind ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini.

It's a group of operators who have built category leaders and want to do it again.

## What's Next

This round of funding lets us double down on our core data and AI platforms, scale our go-to-market efforts, and hire exceptional builders. The future of cybersecurity will be agentic, and we're just getting started.

If you want to see what Cogent can do for your security program, [see the product](https://www.cogent.com/book-demo) for yourself. And if you're excited by the idea of building agentic AI from the ground up while making the digital world safer for everyone, we're hiring across R&D and GTM.

SAN FRANCISCO, Feb. 18, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Cogent Security today announced $42 million in Series A funding led by Bain Capital Ventures, with participation from existing investor Greylock Partners and Definition. Founders and executives from OpenAI, Abnormal Security, and Datadog invested personally. The financing brings Cogent's total funding to $53 million, just six months after the company's official launch.

Why Attackers Are Winning

In 2025, over 48,000 new software vulnerabilities known as CVEs were published. That's 162 percent higher than five years ago. Every enterprise software system has them, and new ones appear constantly as code changes and new threats emerge. Meanwhile, the time needed for hackers to figure out how to exploit new vulnerabilities has collapsed.

Attackers and security teams now operate at completely different speeds. Hackers using AI begin probing enterprises for vulnerabilities minutes after they're first published. Fixing one on the defensive side still takes days or weeks. Security teams hunt down system owners through outdated directories and Slack threads. Developers field tickets without knowing which systems are affected or why this particular issue matters more than the hundreds of others in their queue.

Security backlogs grow faster than teams can clear them. Banks, hospitals, retailers, manufacturers, energy companies—they all face the same problem.

Finding vulnerabilities isn't the hard part. Modern scanning tools handle discovery. The hard part is everything after: figuring out who owns what, understanding real-world risk, coordinating fixes across teams, proving the work was completed. It's slow. It's manual. And the threats now move at machine speed.

"Security teams are drowning in coordination work: chasing down system owners, writing tickets, proving fixes happened. We built AI agents that handle that work end-to-end, so security teams can finally keep pace with attackers," said Vineet Edupuganti, CEO and co-founder of Cogent.

Cogent Security: AI Agents That Automate Remediation

Traditional security tools stop at detection, surfacing findings and leaving humans to handle the rest. Cogent's AI agents investigate vulnerabilities, determine system ownership, assess real-world risk based on the environment, and generate remediation steps ready for engineers to implement. The agents then track fixes through to verified completion. Customers using Cogent have reduced exposure windows for critical risks by an average of 97 percent.

Cogent is built for enterprise governance. Every agent action is traceable and reproducible. The platform integrates directly into existing security and engineering workflows and operates under configurable approval gates and policy enforcement. Organizations can deploy autonomous AI agents without sacrificing control, auditability, or human oversight.

"Cogent has become a meaningful competitive advantage for us."

Vineet led product strategy for a product loved by security teams. Geng built the ML fraud detection system now protecting half of the Fortune 500. After a year running AI agents against 11M+ vulnerabilities in production, six infrastructure lessons on what actually keeps agents safe.

Jan 23, 2026, 9 min read, Geng Sng, Co-Founder and CTO

## The Problem: Agents in High-Stakes Environments

An agent analyzes 11,470,000 vulnerabilities across your infrastructure and decides which to prioritize. It queries production databases, generates remediation plans, and creates tickets for your engineering teams.

This is high-stakes automation. One mistake like querying the wrong tenant's data, leaking credentials in a ticket, or hallucinating a critical severity will have real consequences.

Over the past year at Cogent, we've learned that **building safe agents isn't about better prompts**. It's about infrastructure: isolation boundaries, credential management, and failure containment.

This post shares what we learned—the patterns that work, the ones that don't, and what we're still figuring out.

# Cogent Security

Agentic AI for Cybersecurity Vulnerability Management

It ingests findings from existing scanners and ticketing systems, correlates vulnerabilities to real business risk and asset ownership, and can execute remediation with configurable human-in-the-loop approval gates. The company also develops its own frontier cyber reasoning model, VR-1, purpose-built for autonomous security investigation.
