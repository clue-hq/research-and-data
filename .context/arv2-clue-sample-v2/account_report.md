# Lassie (lassie.ai) — decision-grade account dossier ## 1. IDENTITY AND DISAMBIGUATION
Lassie is the San Francisco-based operating company behind lassie.ai, an AI software company building autonomous administrative systems for independent healthcare practices, initially dental practices. Its public positioning is “AI that runs the doctor’s office”; the product reads insurance claims, reconciles payments, posts to practice ledgers, and flags claims requiring action. The June 3, 2026 financing release identifies the company as “Lassie” and says it is “the company building autonomous systems to run small businesses”; the a16z announcement says it is “starting with dental practices.” Verbatim evidence: “SAN FRANCISCO, Calif.--(BUSINESS WIRE)-- Lassie, the company building autonomous systems to run small businesses, announced it has raised $35 million in Series A financing, bringing its total capital raised to $47 million.” URL: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260603909717/en/Lassie-Raises-%2435M-Led-by-Andreessen-Horowitz-to-Build-AI-for-Small-Businesses-to-Run-Themselves. The company homepage headline is “Lassie — AI That Runs the Doctor's Office.” URL: https://www.lassie.ai/. HQ: San Francisco is explicitly stated in the Business Wire dateline and in the company description returned for the company page. Founding year: NOT FOUND on the reviewed public pages. Legal entity name: NOT FOUND. What it sells: autonomous administrative/insurance and revenue-cycle automation for practices, not pet insurance or veterinary software. Excluded same-name entities: (1) Lassie pet-insurance companies at lassie.co/lassie.se — excluded because they concern pet insurance and are unrelated to healthcare administration; (2) Lassie the fictional collie, film, and TV franchise — excluded because it is entertainment/IP; (3) veterinary, pet-care, dog-related Lassie results — excluded because they do not concern lassie.ai’s San Francisco dental/medical-practice software. No excluded source is used as evidence in this dossier. ## 2. PRODUCT AND TARGET VERTICAL
The product is an autonomous AI agent/system for administrative work in doctors’ offices, with dental as the initial market. The primary workflow evidence is insurance and revenue-cycle administration: reading claims, reconciling payments, posting to the ledger, and identifying exceptions. Exact a16z quote: “Then they spent years building the infrastructure and integrations required for agents to operate directly within a practice’s workflow: reading insurance claims, reconciling payments, posting to the ledger, and flagging claims that need action. Lassie is not a copilot that creates more work for the practice to review. It does the work.” URL: https://a16z.com/announcement/investing-in-lassie/. The company page says: “Lassie works autonomously, around the clock, reading insurance claims, reconciling payments, handling denials and appeals.” URL: https://www.lassie.ai/company. The homepage search result states: “Handling enrollments, converting payments to EFTs, and posting them automatically, without delay.” URL: https://www.lassie.ai/. Practice types: dental is explicitly the starting market; a16z also says the founders spoke with “dentists, primary care doctors, and healthcare finance teams,” indicating medical/primary-care applicability, but a current quantified medical customer base is NOT FOUND. Veterinary service is NOT FOUND and should not be inferred. Exact quote: “Lassie is building AI that runs small businesses, starting with dental practices.”. Named integrations/PMS: public Lassie material explicitly names Dentrix, Eaglesoft, and Open Dental. Quote: “We automate EOB posting and payment matching across Dentrix, Eaglesoft, and Open Dental, without human intervention.” URL: https://www.lassie.ai/blog/ai-dental-admin-support. Pricing: NOT FOUND on the reviewed public pages. Phone handling, phone answering, and scheduling automation: NOT FOUND as confirmed Lassie product capabilities in the reviewed primary sources; do not conflate job-market/blog SEO language with a shipped product claim. ## 3.
