## Description

AI Risk, Governance, and Compliance in 2026: Trust, Verification, and Continuous Monitoring

We sit down with Ed and James from Harmonic Security as we take a look at how AI tooling is being adopted quickly, and the pace of change is creating a growing gap between what an organisation believes is under control and what is actually happening in day-to-day operations. Tools are entering environments through standard routes such as integrations and approvals, but also through unauthorised use, including personal subscriptions and ad hoc uploads of company documents into AI services. This creates exposure that leadership and assurance functions may not have visibility into, even though obligations to customers remain constant.

Recent incidents have intensified scrutiny on third-party risk and assurance. Allegations of invalid SOC 2 certifications and the downstream impact on third parties, subprocessors, and “subprocessors of subprocessors” highlight how quickly risk can propagate when trust is misplaced. The issue is not limited to a single incident; it reflects broader pressure in the market to obtain certifications quickly as a route to enterprise sales.
