## Five things to look for at every company that passes the gates

1. **Does it build its own tool for this job in-house?** Look at its engineering blog, its job posts and talks by its engineers.
2. **Does it already use a competitor's product?** Name the product. Look at press releases, case studies, the competitor's customer pages, and job posts that ask for experience with that product.
3. **When might that contract end?** For example, a deal announced with its length, a government contract award with an end date, or a public request for bids. Say whether the end looks to fall within the next 3 to 6 months.
4. **Are people at the company unhappy with their current tool?** Look for reviews, forum posts or social posts by named employees of this company. A review that does not name the company does not count.
5. **Is there a reason to act now?** Look for a recent breach, ransomware attack or outage; a new security or technology leader in the last 12 months, especially one who came from a company that buys from {seller_name}; a data-centre or cloud migration under way; a regulatory deadline or finding that applies to them; a named Zero Trust or resilience programme; or job posts that show a gap {seller_name} fills. Give the date and source for each.

## Priority

- Mark a company **top** when none of the caveats below applies.
- Mark it **caveat** when it builds its own tool for this job in-house, when it uses a competitor and nothing suggests it wants to switch, or when it recently rolled out a competitor's product.
- A contract that ends within the next 3 to 6 months, or clear unhappiness with the current tool, is an **opportunity**. It keeps a company **top** even when it uses a competitor.
- Write a **remark** of one or two sentences for a seller deciding whom to approach first: why this company is top, or why it is a caveat.

Rank **top** companies by how strong and recent their reasons to act now are, and return them in that order. A company with no reason to act now is still **top** when it fits and none of the caveats apply: keep it, count it, and place it after the ones that have a reason. Never leave a company out because it has no reason to act now.

A caveat company still fits the profile. Keep it in your answer; it simply does not count toward the number asked for.
