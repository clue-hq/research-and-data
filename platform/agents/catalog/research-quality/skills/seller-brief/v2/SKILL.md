# Write the seller brief

You are preparing a brief for the people who will write research pages about target accounts and
the people who work there. Those pages exist for one reason: to help this seller win the target as
a client. Your brief is how the writers learn what the seller sells, why a target might need it and
what kind of public evidence points to that need.

Write it from the seller documents you are given: the company overview, the product material, the
ICP and any persona documents. Use only what those documents say. If something the writers would
need is missing, say so plainly in "What we do not know" instead of filling the gap yourself.

Write in plain sentences. The writers will read this brief before every page, so keep it to what
helps them choose and explain evidence. Around 700 to 1,000 words is usually enough.

## Sections of the brief

Use these headings, in this order.

### What the seller sells

Name the seller and the product or products selected for this project. In two to four sentences,
explain what the product does for a customer, in concrete terms a sales rep would use. If the
product has a specific way of working that matters for a sale (for example, it sits on top of an
existing system, or it requires a data feed), say so.

### What this research is for

One short paragraph: the research helps the seller turn a target account into a client. The pages
should show a rep why this account is worth pursuing, why now, who to talk to first and what to
lead with.

### Problems the offer solves, and the evidence that points to them

This is the most important section. List the problems the product solves for a customer. For each
problem, list the kinds of public evidence that would suggest a target has that problem or is
likely to care about it. Be concrete about the evidence: the kind of document, statement, job
posting, filing, workforce pattern or event a researcher could actually find.

Use this shape for each problem:

- **Problem:** what goes wrong for a customer without the product.
- **Evidence that suggests it:** the public signals, as specific as the documents allow.
- **Why it matters to the buyer:** the outcome the buyer cares about, in their terms.

### What makes a target a strong fit

Summarise the ICP's hard requirements, preferences and exclusions in plain language. Keep numbers,
units and thresholds exactly as the ICP states them. Separate requirements from preferences.

### Proof the seller can use

List the proof points the documents support: named customers, published results, guarantees,
awards. Say who published each one. Do not turn a marketing claim into an independent result.

### Who buys and who uses it

From the persona documents or, if there are none, from the company and ICP material: the roles that
usually decide, pay for, evaluate and use the product, and what each of them cares about. Say which
source this comes from.

### Competitors and alternatives

What the target may already have instead of this product, and how the seller says it differs.
Writers use this to avoid pitching against something the target is happy with.

### What we do not know

Gaps in the seller material that limit the pages, such as a missing persona document or no stated
pricing.
