# A page for one person at an account

Everything in `one-to-one.md` applies: read it first. This file says only what changes.

## What drives the page

- The person's own sourced professional criterion (something they have said they need to get right
  at work) or, failing that, a documented responsibility. A job title alone supports framing for
  the role, not their own priorities.
- Compared with a page for their account, something real must change: the explanation, the
  priority, the depth or the evidence. A greeting on top of the account page is not a page for this
  person.
- Before you build, ask yourself: which professional evidence about this person changed the page?
  If you cannot name it, build a page aimed at their role (`one-to-one.md`, "The reader"), and say
  in your report that it is aimed at their role rather than built from what is known of them.
- Never use private or sensitive interests, guessed emotional pressure ("needs a quick win") or
  assumed budget authority. A hobby is not a hook.

## What to read

The account's research, and what the brief and the research say about this person: their role and
any sourced statement of theirs. When there is nothing about them beyond a name and a title, the
page is aimed at their role, as above.

## Naming the reader

- The reader may be named discreetly, for example in an eyebrow above the headline: "For Morgan Lee,
  engineering systems". Never in the header's line or as a menu label.
- The headline is about the professional value, not their name.
- Name no one else at the account. Never generate a picture of them.

## Proof, identity and action

As `one-to-one.md`: the short line near customer proof, "Prepared for" and the account in the
header with the account's logo only there, and the next step the brief names.

## An example (made up; take inspiration from it, never copy its words or use its facts)

The example is about an imaginary software seller, Keel, and an imaginary account, Aster.

The account page for Aster is about consistent release signoff across the units it acquired, and
shows the wider review flow. Morgan leads engineering-systems assessment there and has said, in a
sourced statement, that they want clear ownership at handoffs.

Morgan's page focuses on one handoff: who is named at each approval, and which checklist version
applies. Headline: "Make the next signoff owner clear." It never says "we know you struggle with
handoffs", and it does not assume Morgan controls the budget.
