# A page for one account

## The reader

A page written for one company, and usually for one reader there: the role the brief names
(an operations lead, a CFO), shaped by the brief's notes on who decides. Never name or describe
individual people at the account. A page for one named person is a contact page (`contact.md`).

They usually do not know the seller yet, and may not see their situation as one the seller can help
with. Open on their situation, in their words, not on the seller.

When the brief aims the page at a role at the account, shape the priorities, depth and proof for
that role's work, still without naming anyone.

## What to read

- `research/overview.md`, `research/insights.md`, and the claims behind what you use in
  `research/claims.md`. How to read the research and its tags: `evidence` ("Reading the research").
- **The insight**: something the account has said or done that the offer speaks to: a budget line,
  a plan, a project, a hire, a stated goal. Its size or location is not an insight. Prefer the
  account's own documents (budget, plans, filings, its website) over press about it. Choose one
  insight, and two or three priorities the research supports.
- The seller's product documents and its published case studies (`evidence`, "Customer proof").
- The seller's own sections come last as a source of facts. Before you use one of their numbers,
  customer names or quotes here, decide while you write whether it fits this page as it is or needs
  rewording (`evidence`, "Where facts come from").

## Words and tone

`page-copy` applies throughout. What is particular to this kind:
- Use the account's own phrases word for word where the research quotes them.
- What the page says about the account's outcome is a "could": a plain-words implication of what
  the seller does, joined to their situation. Never a number, percentage, money amount or time saved
  for them, and never "will" about their result (`page-copy`, "Claims").
- Each benefit statement has a bridge line in the plan (`planning-a-page`, "The plan format").
- The hero describes what the seller proposes to put in place for them, not a result.
- Positive framing: never describe the account's own product or setup negatively; say what the
  seller adds. Only the relationship words `page-copy` allows ("Talking about the prospect").
- Use the account's own figures sparingly, only when one sharpens the insight.

## Shape

Often five to seven sections, built around decision material: read `decision-material.md`. A usual
order, as a guide: what this is, for this account; their priorities, each joined to what the seller
does; how it could work for them; proof; the offer.

The "how it works" section frames itself through its heading, for example "How it could work at
Lakeside" (Lakeside standing for the account's name). The heading carries the "could". Add no disclaimer, no line saying it is a working model
and no line denying a partnership: the header's "Prepared for" already frames the page.

## Proof

Named customers' results, each attributed in the same line (`page-copy`, "Claims"). Where customer
proof sits beside material about the account, add one short line near it saying these are results
each named customer reported, not a promise about this account's outcome. Word it yourself. An
example, made up for inspiration only (Lakeside stands for the account): "Results reported by each
named customer. They show what the approach did there, not a forecast for Lakeside." This is the only qualification the page carries on purpose.

## Identity, header and footer

- The header is the account's one place: "Prepared for" and the account's full name, with the
  account's logo beside the seller's. The account's logo appears nowhere else on the page. The hero
  names the account in words. No second logo, no band of the account's branding.
- The seller's header, trimmed: rebuilt with its look and layout, its menus and links removed, the
  seller's logo and one button kept, and the "Prepared for" line and the account's logo added. The
  plain footer. (`building-with-puck`, "The header and footer"; logos: `imagery-and-media`, "Logos".)

## The action

The next step the brief names (a meeting, an assessment, a proposal), at its real scope, worded as
what they receive. No links that take the reader away; a customer story you cite may open in a new
tab.

## The hero

Usually the seller's home page hero, adapted (`page-design`, "Which hero").

## Checking it

`page-review`, with the account checks: the swap test, the proof line, no people named.
