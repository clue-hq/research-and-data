# A page for a whole audience

## The reader

An audience or segment (freight brokers, finance leaders at mid-size retailers), often arriving from an ad or a campaign
email. They usually know the kind of solution they are looking for: open on the seller's approach to
their shared problem.

## What to read

The confirmed ICP and personas in `seller/`, the seller's product documents, and the seller's own
sections. Run a few `web_search`es for a little context about the audience. The ICP and personas are
the seller's view of an audience, never facts about a particular company (`evidence`, "Reading the
research").

## Words and tone

- The audience's shared problem, in broad language and in the segment's own vocabulary (from the ICP
  and persona documents).
- What the page says about the audience's outcome is a "could", as on every page (`page-copy`,
  "Claims"). Never write as if every visitor had been researched.

## Shape

Often five to seven sections. The decision material is lighter than on a page for an account: show
the product doing its job for this audience (a walkthrough, a real product state, attributed proof),
not a list of features.

A generated setting for the segment's industry is a normal use of a generated image
(`imagery-and-media`, "Generating an image").

## Identity, header and footer

- No account line and no account's logo. The hero names the audience.
- The seller's header and footer, cloned as they are: its menu and footer links lead to the seller's
  real pages. The header's button goes to the page's primary action.
- How to set either up: `building-with-puck` ("The header and footer").

## The action

The demo, trial, contact or resource the brief names. A few secondary links that help the decision
(the product page, pricing) are fine.

## The hero

Usually the seller's home page hero, adapted (`page-design`, "Which hero").
