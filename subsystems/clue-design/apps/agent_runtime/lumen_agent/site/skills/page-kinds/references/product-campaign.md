# A product campaign page

## The reader

Anyone arriving from an ad or an email about one product or one offer. They usually know the
product, or are ready to act.

## Match what they clicked

Continue the ad's or email's offer and wording, when the brief supplies them, so the reader sees at
once that they are in the right place. Facts lean on the seller's files and its own sections,
especially its product page.

## Shape

The shortest kind: the promise, the proof, case studies or testimonials about this product, and the
action. When the seller has only one product, add a customer logo wall and similar proof. A logo
wall sits beside the argument; it never replaces it.

The decision material is light: show the product doing its job, not a list of features.

## Screenshots

Pair each product screenshot with what it shows. Look at it first (`view_asset`), then write one
line in the plan: the reader's task → what the product does → what is visible in the screenshot →
the sentence beside it. The rules for screenshots themselves are in `imagery-and-media`
("Screenshots").

## Identity, header and footer

- No account markers; the hero names the offer.
- The seller's header, trimmed to its logo and one button (no menu, no line), and the plain footer
  (`building-with-puck`, "The header and footer").
- One or two links to the product page or pricing, in the page, only when they help the decision.

## The action

The offer the ad or email made, worded the way they worded it.

## The hero

Usually the seller's product page hero, adapted (`page-design`, "Which hero").
