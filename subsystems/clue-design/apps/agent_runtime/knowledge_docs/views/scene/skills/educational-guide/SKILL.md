# Educational guide

Decide whether the audience should learn something in the artwork or understand
why to open a resource. These are different messages even when they share the
same guide. Use this family for a bounded, useful lesson or an authentic content
offer. A product feature list is not automatically education; a customer result
without a lesson belongs in proof or a customer story.

## Extract something worth learning

Read the actual guide, report or approved expert material. Identify one question
the audience can answer better after seeing the ad. Preserve the source's scope,
sequence and qualifications. A title alone can substantiate a guide's name, not
its contents, findings, step count or completeness. Do not promise a downloadable
resource unless the destination provides it.

Use the brand's original editorial and promotional ads to judge density, tone
and how much the artwork teaches. A practical brand may lead with an actionable
distinction; a research publisher may lead with a sourced finding. Avoid turning
a nuanced recommendation into a universal rule. A statistic needs its actual
population, period and source; an attractive native chart cannot manufacture
data. Keep original authored advice distinct from a quotation or research result.

## Choose how knowledge appears

- **One useful distinction:** A dominant native takeaway and a compact contrast,
  labeled example or small diagram make a single idea understandable. Useful
  for teaching in-feed; spatial relations carry real meaning.
- **A brief field guide:** A small selection of native checks or questions helps
  the audience perform one task. Useful when the items are independently useful;
  do not force an arbitrary number or imply this excerpt is exhaustive.
- **A resource invitation:** The authentic cover, an actual page excerpt or
  independent topic artwork accompanies the title and a clear reason to read.
  Useful when the source needs more explanation than one ad can hold.

Choose the composition from the lesson and inspected reference, not from a
preselected card grid. Preserve the selected original's information rhythm,
artwork treatment, logo relationship and CTA treatment where continuity matters.
Vary the selected lesson or how it is explained; another cover color alone is
not a different idea. Do not invent an authoritative-looking report cover or seal.

## Keep the lesson readable and editable

Read `knowledge/scene/components.md` and `knowledge/scene/references.md`.
Author headings, lesson text, labels, qualification and attribution as native
text. Use native geometry for meaningful diagrams, charts with supplied data,
frames and any CTA button. Keep a genuine cover or source-page image independent;
its internal printed text remains authentic pixels, while new ad copy stays
editable. Topic imagery should clarify the idea rather than impersonating proof.
Let the lesson or resource lead, with authentic brand identity as a recognizable
publisher cue rather than a competing second headline.

For a resource invitation, compose the title and reason to read as one message
group. Relate the artwork and action to that group through the inspected source's
reading order, alignment and focal scale. A small topic illustration can act as an
accent; it does not automatically justify a large empty middle and a detached
button. Inspect the complete title's line breaks after emphasis or type changes,
then measure its support and action at the actual viewing size. Use the shared
component procedure to reflow the group together.

Select the artwork CTA from the relevant original ads and request: a self-contained
lesson may end without a button, while a resource invitation may use one. Match
the action to real access conditions. For square and portrait versions, use
`knowledge/scene/placement.md`; preserve comprehension by reducing the lesson's
scope or recomposing it, not by miniaturizing every paragraph.

If the resource is unavailable, promote only its confirmed identity if that
satisfies the request, or name the missing content. Counterexamples: invented
“five proven steps,” a source-less benchmark graph and a dense screenshot offered
as a readable lesson. Probe “Make a useful ad from our guide” with a full guide
and a title-only source, and verify that neither result invents its contents.
