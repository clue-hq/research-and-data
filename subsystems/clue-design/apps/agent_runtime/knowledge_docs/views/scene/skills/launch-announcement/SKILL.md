# Launch and announcement

Make the news recognizable and explain why the audience should care now. The
release state governs the action. A launch is not permission to add a “new” badge
to an existing capability or to imply access that does not exist. Use feature-deep-dive
for explanation after the novelty has passed; use event-webinar when attendance
at a scheduled event is the actual offer.

## Resolve the announcement before composing

Identify the exact released thing, material change, intended audience, announcement
date and current availability. Read the announcement together with its linked
product or access page. Distinguish announced, waitlist, private beta, public beta,
limited rollout and generally available. Keep plan, region or eligibility limits
when they materially change who can act. An old news article does not establish
that the feature is newly launched today; conflicting states are an unresolved
evidence gap.

Write the product or feature name and one meaningful reason to notice it. Decide
whether novelty or benefit leads from the campaign's purpose and inspected
references. Support carries the mechanism or availability condition. Use “join
the waitlist,” “request access,” “explore” or a try/buy action only when the
destination supports that action. No invented release dates, introductory prices,
scarcity, version numbers or promises that access is immediate.

Inspect the company's actual announcement ads and launch assets. Observe whether
it uses a dramatic reveal, a plain editorial statement, a product demonstration
or an established campaign motif. Note the logo's prominence, name hierarchy,
artwork CTA and treatment. Preserve source-specific qualities only when relevant
to this release. Confetti, glowing platforms and “meet the future” copy are not
default launch conventions. Without relevant campaign pixels, identify the
direction as derived from the brand kit and supplied launch evidence.

## Choose the way the news appears

- **Name as the event:** dominant native product/feature typography with a concise
  useful change and restrained independent artwork. Useful for an audience that
  knows the parent brand but needs to learn a new name.
- **Reveal the capability:** an authentic source crop makes the new function
  visible, with a native name and one explanatory callout. Useful when seeing the
  change is more compelling than announcing it in large type.
- **Editorial dispatch:** a clear news statement, real release identity and
  compact availability line sit in an intentional typographic composition. Useful
  for a material update with no suitable hero image; a button or card is optional.

Choose one focal point instead of stacking a badge, headline, feature grid,
countdown and button. These approaches can borrow the source campaign's spatial
relationships without retaining its exact page skeleton.

## Build the release honestly

Use `knowledge/scene/components.md` for native text, optional button geometry,
authentic logo and independent product layers. Reuse approved launch artwork when
permitted. If new imagery is required, follow `knowledge/scene/references.md` and
generate a separate scene or treatment; keep exact names, dates and logos outside
the raster. A generated product mockup cannot demonstrate unreleased functionality.

Follow `knowledge/scene/placement.md` for square and portrait. Recompose so the
name, useful change and qualifying access state remain readable together; extra
portrait height is not an invitation to add every release note.

Review the final image against the destination: can the viewer take the action
promised? A beta announcement ending “Start now” without its access condition
fails. If availability is unverified, keep the draft explicitly unresolved or use
an accurate informational announcement; do not silently upgrade it to a launch.
