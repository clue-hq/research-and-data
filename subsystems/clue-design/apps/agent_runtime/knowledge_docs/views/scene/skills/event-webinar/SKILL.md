# Event and webinar

An event creative sells a reason to attend or watch. Use this family when a
specific event and its state matter to the message. An undated product idea is
positioning; a useful extract from a past talk may be educational-guide. Do not
force a speaker grid onto an event whose topic or visual identity is stronger.

## Verify the invitation

Read the current event source, not just its original launch ad. Determine whether
it is upcoming, live, registration-closed, cancelled or available on demand.
Verify the displayed title, date, time zone, location/online status, access terms,
speakers, affiliations and destination. Use the source's exact local time and
zone unless a reliable conversion is available. Historical references can guide
design without supplying current dates or registration claims.

Choose the audience's reason to join: a question answered, a useful practice,
access to particular expertise or a shared experience. Specificity beats “Don't
miss out.” Let the original event campaign and brand voice determine whether
copy feels like a considered invitation, lively community gathering or focused
technical session. Keep speaker credentials factual and proportionate to their
role; appearing in an old photo does not establish this year's participation.

## Choose what attracts attention

- **Topic-led invitation:** Express the central question in native type, with a
  compact event identity and essential logistics. Useful when the content is the
  main draw or speaker photography is weak.
- **People-led conversation:** Authentic portraits support the relation between
  named participants and a clear topic. Useful when recognizable expertise gives
  the audience a reason to join; not every participant needs equal visual weight.
- **Event-world composition:** Use the event's distinctive independent artwork
  or authentic venue image as the focal experience, with native invitation copy.
  Useful when a recurring conference has an established visual identity.

Choose from evidence and references, rather than assigning one composition to
all webinars. Preserve the original's typography, image character, event-brand
relationship and CTA treatment when continuity is requested. A replay can retain
the campaign world while changing its invitation and removing obsolete urgency.

## Keep the invitation editable

Read `knowledge/scene/components.md` and `knowledge/scene/references.md`.
Headlines, dates, time zones, credentials and button labels stay native. Buttons
use native geometry; portraits, logos and event artwork are separate authentic
or appropriately generated image layers. Do not generate a replacement likeness
or bake the speaker roster into new artwork. Authored diagrams must describe
supported talk content, never fictional event outcomes.

The main brand, event identity and co-host marks have different jobs. Use their
supplied roles and lockup rules to establish hierarchy, without manufacturing
sponsorship. Follow the relevant original ads and user request for artwork CTA
presence and style; choose registration or replay wording from the actual
destination. A native platform event format and a square event-promotion image
are different placements. Use `knowledge/scene/placement.md` for their constraints
and for portrait recomposition; protect faces and readable event details.

If event facts cannot be confirmed, retain only supported announcement content
when useful and name the missing logistics. If the requested invitation cannot
be made truthfully, report the gap. Counterexamples include replay artwork with
“Register now,” invented scarcity and a four-person grid of unsupported speakers.
Probe “Promote our webinar” against both an upcoming page and a replay page;
the useful reason to watch should survive while the action changes accurately.
