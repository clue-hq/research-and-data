# Case-study narrative

Show what changed for a real customer and enough of how it happened to make the
result meaningful. Use attributed-proof when a quote or number can stand alone;
use customer-spotlight when the relationship or customer's work is the subject.
This family needs a story supported by evidence, not a testimonial invented around
a customer logo.

## Extract a truthful story

Read the complete relevant story, not only its hero metric. Identify the customer,
initial situation, action or implementation, observed outcome and essential
conditions. Bind those statements separately to evidence. Distinguish the
customer's report, the vendor's interpretation and a measured experiment. A
sequence of events does not establish that the product caused everything that
happened afterward.

Select one useful transformation for the intended audience. Write the headline
around the change; support supplies the mechanism and context. Preserve units,
timeframe, affected team and qualifying language. An implementation that combined
new processes, staffing and software cannot be rewritten as a software-only
guarantee. Company revenue or valuation mentioned nearby is not automatically a
product outcome. Quotes remain exact or faithful marked excerpts under the shared
proof rules; paraphrases have no quotation marks.

Inspect the advertiser's actual customer-story ads and the customer's approved
source assets. Observe who is visually central, how much context accompanies the
result, how attribution is treated, and whether the action invites reading or a
product demo. Keep campaign treatment separate from customer evidence. The
presence of a customer logo in a reference does not authorize a new endorsement.
When campaign pixels are unavailable, record that limitation rather than claiming
to reproduce an established customer-ad style.

## Give the story an appropriate shape

- **Outcome with a visible bridge:** a dominant native result is immediately
  paired with a short implementation statement and readable attribution. Useful
  when the outcome is strong but would mislead without its cause and scope.
- **The changed situation:** an authentic customer or product image leads, with
  concise native challenge/action/result text organized around the image. Useful
  when the work itself makes the story more distinctive than a number.
- **Editorial excerpt:** a substantial exact quote or sourced narrative sentence
  anchors a typographic composition; a smaller contextual line supplies the
  action or result. Useful for a qualitative story with a strong human account.

These are choices, not mandatory three-card timelines. If the story needs more
space than one static placement permits, select a narrower slice or record that a
multi-page format is needed; do not compress every stage into unreadable labels.

## Preserve evidence through production

Read `knowledge/scene/components.md` for native result, quote, qualifier and
attribution layers, authentic independent logos and optional CTA construction.
Reuse approved customer photography or authentic product images when useful.
Follow `/.deepagent/skills/generative-imagery/SKILL.md` for separately generated contextual artwork.
Generated art may illustrate a concept; it cannot depict a fabricated customer
event, employee, quotation, dashboard result or endorsement as real evidence.

Use a read-the-story action when the destination is the case study; do not promise
that viewers will achieve the same result. Follow `knowledge/scene/placement.md`
for square/portrait recomposition. Keep result and essential qualification within
the same readable relationship, even when imagery moves or the reading order changes.

Review the final creative for a truthful challenge-to-action-to-result link. A
large percentage with the customer's name but no explanation fails this narrative
job. If only a verified quote remains, use attributed-proof. If no approved
customer evidence is usable, switch to supported product positioning and report
the missing story rather than completing it with plausible details.
