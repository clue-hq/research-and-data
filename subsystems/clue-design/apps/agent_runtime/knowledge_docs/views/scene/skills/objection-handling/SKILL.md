# Objection handling

Answer one real concern with a precise reason to reconsider. The concern should
come from the brief, approved audience research or relevant product context, not
from inventing what a prospect fears. Use comparison-alternatives when the decision
requires comparing products; use offer-conversion when the offer itself is the
reason to act. This family is not a checklist of every possible objection.

## Bound the question and the answer

Identify the decision at risk, the exact concern and the smallest supported answer
that materially addresses it. Separate “can this connect to our system?” from
“will our migration be effortless?”; compatibility evidence answers only the
first. For security or privacy, read the policy for the named feature and current
plan. A narrow data-training statement is not a guarantee about every kind of
processing, retention or third-party access.

Map the answer to product documentation, implementation evidence, an approved
customer account or actual offer terms. Record material limits, prerequisites and
unresolved conditions with the claim. If the evidence answers only part of the
objection, narrow the wording to that part. If it does not answer the concern,
make an informational next step or keep the draft unresolved; “contact sales”
cannot stand in for an unsubstantiated reassuring headline.

Inspect the brand's actual concern-addressing ads, product pages and trust or
implementation material. Note whether the tone is direct, explanatory, reassuring
or conversational, and how proof, logo and action are presented. A reference's
confident tone does not expand the evidence. Do not convert another brand's badge,
certification or customer quote into a generic visual cue for trust.

Write the headline as a useful answer when the audience already knows the concern.
Use a question when it genuinely helps frame the issue, keeping it neutral rather
than alarming. Support explains the mechanism and important limit. “No risk,”
“zero effort” and “works with everything” require their own evidence; a friendly
tone or small-print exception cannot make those absolutes accurate.

## Select a convincing relationship

- **Answer first:** a clear native statement leads, with a compact evidence or
  mechanism line close beside it. Useful for a narrow concern such as a documented
  control or supported integration. No question bubble is required.
- **Show the control:** an authentic UI detail or simple native process explains
  how the user retains agency, configures access or begins adoption. Useful when
  tangible behavior is more convincing than reassurance.
- **Concern and response:** a short, neutral question sits in deliberate relation
  to its scoped answer and source context. Useful when ambiguity in the question
  itself needs resolving. This need not look like a chat transcript or FAQ card.

Choose from the reference treatment and audience knowledge, not an obligatory
shield icon or row of check marks. A lock illustration is atmosphere, not evidence.

## Build, adapt and challenge

Use `knowledge/scene/components.md` for native text, qualification, optional CTA
geometry, authentic logo and independently placeable product evidence. Reuse
suitable source imagery; requested new artwork is generated separately under
`/.deepagent/skills/generative-imagery/SKILL.md`. Never generate certification seals, fake support
messages or fabricated customer proof. Typography alone may carry a supported
answer when imagery is optional.

Follow `knowledge/scene/placement.md` for square/portrait adaptation. Keep the
answer, mechanism and material condition legible together; reduce decorative
content before sacrificing qualification. Choose an action that resolves the
remaining uncertainty, such as exploring a real security or implementation guide.
Review as a skeptical buyer: does the evidence answer this exact concern, and
does the destination continue that answer? If either fails, repair the claim or
report the gap instead of adding more visual reassurance.
